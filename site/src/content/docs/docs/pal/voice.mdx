## The reply pipeline

Stages 1–4 are soft: a failure triggers one regeneration. Stages 5 and 6 are hard: they hold the reply for you. The rules in the prompt are advice; these stages are enforced in code.

| # | Stage | What it checks | On failure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Draft | Purpose-keyed session, web search on, read-only tools | — |
| 2 | Bot-tell guard | Stock phrases, leaked reasoning, denials of your own facts, a reply that repeats the shape of the last three | Regenerate |
| 3 | Voice floor | Stylometric distance from your own sent messages | Regenerate |
| 4 | Critique-refine | MEDIUM/LOW drafts only, edited against your 5 most similar real messages | Keep the original if the edit is not closer to your voice |
| 5 | Commitment gate | Promises of money or an action, in any language | [Hold for you](/docs/pal/human-in-the-loop/) |
| 6 | Grounding gate | Facts about your plans pal can't verify; bad news answered in the wrong tone | [Hold for you](/docs/pal/human-in-the-loop/) |
| 7 | Claim rewrite | "I sent it" becomes "I'll send it" unless the action was recorded for that contact in the last 10 minutes | Rewrite |
| 8 | Paced send | Your real reply latency for that contact, jittered, clamped to 600 ms – 25 s | — |

## How the voice floor works

The floor is fitted in-process on your own sent messages (at least 50 samples). A draft further from your style than 95% of your real messages is suppressed as `voice-drift` and regenerated once, with a hint about which feature drifted. Every contact draft logs `voice score {delta, floor, ok}`, so you can see it working.

It scores surface style, not meaning. It never runs on your own chat, on groups, or on one- and two-word replies.

## Teach it with 👎

1. Tap 👎 on a reply. The inbound message, the bad reply and your better reply (if you send one) are logged.
2. Patterns that recur across three or more rejections graduate into **style lessons**, written to the `## Style lessons (managed)` section of your owner profile.
3. Lessons are injected into every future prompt, for every contact.

An LLM clustering pass also finds patterns the fixed rules miss. It can only add or raise a lesson, never remove one, and any error falls back to the deterministic pass.

## Tell it facts

Your owner profile (`~/.pal/owner-profile.md`, or `$PAL_OWNER_PROFILE`) holds facts pal must never contradict and per-contact naming rules:

```markdown
## Facts
- spouse: Sam
- wedding anniversary: 2017-06-03

## Relationships
- Sujith: college friend | address as: Sujith | never: bava
```

You can also teach it in your own chat: "remember: anniversary is June 3 2017" or "don't call Sujith bava". pal acknowledges with `📝 noted — …`.

## Settings

| Variable | Default | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `PAL_VOICE_FLOOR` | on | `0` disables the stylometric floor |
| `PAL_VOICE_REFINE` | on | `0` disables critique-refine |
| `PAL_DISLIKE_LLM_CLUSTER` | on | `0` keeps only the deterministic lesson miner |
