PAL is two products on one platform. **pal** is a personal agent that answers your iMessage and WhatsApp threads as you, from your Mac, and holds anything that commits you until you say yes. **PAL Runtime** is the platform pal runs on: a runtime for AI agents that makes runs **durable**, **budgeted** and **sandboxed**. pal talks to the same sessions API you use for your own agents.

| | pal | PAL Runtime |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | A personal agent for your own iMessage and WhatsApp | A platform for running agents, yours or pal's |
| Who it is for | You, on your Mac | Developers and platform teams |
| What it does | Drafts, checks and sends replies in your voice; holds the risky ones | Runs agents durably, within budget, with untrusted code in a microVM |
| Where it runs | Two channel bridges on your Mac, next to a control plane | Your laptop, your cluster, or a managed control plane with the data plane in your VPC |
| Start with | [How pal works](/docs/pal/) | [How PAL Runtime works](/docs/architecture/overview/) |

## What PAL is not

- **Not a chat client.** pal acts through the iMessage and WhatsApp accounts on your Mac. It does not replace either app.
- **Not fully unattended.** pal sends ordinary replies on its own. It holds anything that commits you, and it does not verify a held claim; it holds it.
- **Not a hosted service you sign up for.** A fully managed PAL cloud is the intended offering, not a shipped one. Today you run the control plane yourself ([Deploy to the cloud](/docs/start/deploy/)).
- **Not a model.** You bring provider keys. Agents ask for a capability and the model router picks the model.
